Every night, the natural darkness of the sky is being washed out by artificial light. The same natural darkness nocturnal wildlife has relied on for millions of years. Research shows a very considerable portion of the world lives under a heavily artificial light-polluted night sky. This is a major problem for nocturnal animals as it directly interferes with their mating, hunting, and navigation behaviors built around the true darkness of night. This pollution affects all ranges of nocturnal life. From sea turtle hatchlings instinctively crawling towards the brightest horizon, following the moonlight that reflects off the ocean. To the faint signals of precisely timed patterns of flashes fireflies use to find a mate. Despite how widespread this problem is, most people have never encountered the term "light pollution" outside of trying to find their favorite constellation in the night sky. Having no idea the effects it has on wildlife at all.

Light pollution is a well documented problem. There are several organizations with excellent informational resources regarding the matter. DarkSky International, the ALAN (Artificial Light At Night) Database, and light pollution maps to name a few. However, these all have one major flaw in common. By providing information without detailing the problems caused by our carelessness, these websites fail to motivate the general population to take a stand on this issue. Light pollution maps like LightPollutionMap.app, give wonderful insight on how widespread the problem is but don't go further. DarkSky International is an exceptionally well defined organization driven to document where light pollution is, but that doesn't touch on the effects light pollution has on wildlife. Although all of this extraordinarily well curated information is useful, it fails to captivate the reader or give detail on how each individual can make a difference. The ALAN database might be the best resource regarding light pollution at night when it comes to actually studying the matter, but even this fails to provide a normal reader with digestible information on how detrimental this issue truly is to nocturnal animals. The overarching problem with all of these sites is lack of direct advocacy for the animals and instead a focus on astronomy or pure data. There is no resource available connecting how light pollution exists to the effects it has on these creatures in these environments. This site fills that gap. It connects these dots allowing the everyman to have a personal connection to the animals suffering from the effects of light pollution. It gives a starting point for someone with zero background in the matter to understand both the scope of the problem and its specific effects on the nocturnal animals that depend on the darkness of the night to survive.
