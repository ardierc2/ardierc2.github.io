Opossum

Quick Facts

  • Size: 14-18 inches
  • Weight: 4-14 pounds
  • Lifespan: 2-4 years
  • Conservation Status: Least Concern
  • Range: North and South America

Key Nighttime Adaptations

Opossums have naturally massive pupils, allowing them to see in great detail at night, while having poor vision during the day leading to damage from bright lights. They also possess impressive senses of hearing and smell, which can be overwhelmed in the daytime, but a powerful tool at night when noise is low, and smells have grown faint.

Diet

Opossums are omnivorous and scavengers, feeding on fruits, insects, small animals, and human food scraps.

Role in Ecosystem

As opossums are one of the primary tick eaters in their ecosystem, they help control tick populations and reduce the spread of tick-borne diseases. Additionally, their scavenging habits contribute to the decomposition of organic matter, maintaining ecological balance.

Fun Fact: Opossums are the only marsupial (pouched mammal) native to North America.

Light Pollution's Effect on Opossums

Light pollution causes opossums' normal schedule to be disrupted, leading to increased daytime activity. This overwhelms their senses that are delicately tuned to the night. They can suffer eye damage from bright lights and daytime movement. Their daytime activity can cause the spread of disease and stress for humans as well as the animals.
